A con man sold the Eiffel Tower. Twice.
In nineteen twenty-five, Paris was tired of painting it.
Victor Lustig forged a government letter and invited six scrap dealers to a hotel suite.
He told them the city had secretly decided to tear the tower down.
Too much rust.
Ten thousand tons of iron, sold from a hotel room with a fountain pen.
One dealer paid him a fortune and stayed quiet — because who reports buying a monument under the table?
That silence bought him a second sale.
Then the second buyer called the police.
He ran to America, escaped down a rope, and got caught anyway.
The tower never moved an inch.
